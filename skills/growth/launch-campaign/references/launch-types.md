# Launch types

Size the campaign to the thing. Over-launching a small change teaches the audience to
ignore announcements; under-launching a product wastes the one day people will pay
attention. These are starting points: the audience evidence and `learnings.md` override them.

| Type | Timeline to use | Primary asset | Usual primary channel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product launch (first public release) | full: T-14 to T+14 | a demo that shows the result in under 30 s | where the audience already gathers; one launch community at most |
| Mobile app release | full, anchored to store approval | store listing (screenshots, preview video) plus short videos | short video, the store itself, owned list |
| Feature | T-3 to T+7 | before/after or demo clip | existing users first (in-app, email, changelog), then social |
| Redesign | T-7 to T+7 | before/after with the reason for the change | existing users and followers |
| New market or language | T-14 to T+14 in that market only | natively made assets for that market | the market's own platforms and communities |
| Milestone (users, funding, anniversary) | launch day to T+3 | a story with a lesson and one true number | the founder's own channel |

## Product launch

- **T-14 to T-7:** warm an audience that can show up on the day: waitlist, build-in-public
  posts, early users asked for honest feedback (and for permission to quote it). No
  audience at all means the first job is finding twenty people, not scheduling posts.
- **Launch day:** one push on the primary channel with the owner replying all day.
- **After:** the launch post is not the end. T+1 thanks and first numbers (real ones),
  T+3 answers the top objection that came up, T+7 a lesson or a user story, T+14 a review
  of what worked that feeds `learnings.md`.
- **Trap:** launching everywhere on the same hour with the same text.

## Mobile app release

- The date belongs to the store. Submit early, treat approval as a dependency, and keep the
  timeline relative until the build is approved. Phased rollouts move "launch day".
- The store listing is a launch asset: first two screenshots carry the claim, the preview
  video is a demo, the description opens with what the user gets. Screenshots are real
  screens. Localised listings are written natively.
- Ratings and reviews are asked for inside the app after a successful moment, never
  bought, traded or written by the team.
- **Trap:** driving traffic before the listing converts, or before analytics can attribute
  installs.

## Feature

- Existing users are the audience that matters most: in-app note, changelog entry, one
  email to the people the feature is for. Public posts come second and show the feature
  solving a named problem.
- One strong clip beats five announcement posts. Skip T-14 and T-7 entirely; a teaser is
  only worth it if users have been asking for this.
- **Trap:** announcing with the feature's internal name instead of what it lets people do.

## Redesign

- Lead with why, not with "new look". Show the old and new side by side and name what got
  easier. Tell existing users before they are surprised; give them where-did-it-go answers.
- Expect complaints; plan the T+1 and T+3 replies in advance. Do not argue taste.
- **Trap:** a redesign with no user-visible benefit is a changelog line, not a campaign.

## New market or language

- Treat it as a product launch for that market: check the claim still holds there
  (pricing, payment methods, regulation, cultural fit), list local alternatives, and use
  local channels and communities rather than translating the home-market plan.
- Assets are written natively per the shared `languages.md`; visuals show that market's
  settings; right-to-left layouts are designed, not mirrored.
- **Trap:** announcing availability before support, billing and the store listing work in
  that market.

## Milestone

- Only numbers the owner supplied, exact and dated. The post earns attention by what was
  learned on the way, not by the number. Thank specific people with their permission.
- **Trap:** a milestone with nothing useful for the reader; it reads as bragging.

## Launch communities

Always read the shared `platform-playbook.md` and the community's current rules first; the
notes below only say what to plan for.

- **Product Hunt:** worth it when the audience is makers, early adopters or other founders,
  and the product can be tried the same day. Plan a maker comment that tells the story, a
  gallery of real screens, and the owner's availability for the whole day in the site's own
  time zone. Never ask for upvotes; asking for honest feedback is fine. A weak fit is a
  skipped channel, not a box to tick.
- **Hacker News:** for technical products with something a developer can inspect: source,
  docs, a write-up of how it works. Plain title, no superlatives, the founder answering
  questions with specifics. Not for consumer apps or announcements with no substance.
- **Reddit:** only in communities where the account already has standing or the rules
  explicitly allow launch posts. Value-first post in the community's own register, full
  disclosure of affiliation, the owner present in the comments. Use `community-growth` to
  check rules and standing; a removed post on launch day is a planned-for risk.
- **Discord / Slack groups:** announce in the designated channel only, once, and stay to
  answer.
