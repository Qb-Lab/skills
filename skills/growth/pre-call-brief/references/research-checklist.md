# Research checklist

Ordered by value. Work top down and stop when the time budget is spent. Every item yields
either a fact with a source or a one-word "not found". Public pages only; never log in,
sign up, or submit anything.

## 1. The booking form or message (always first, one minute)

- Name, email domain, company if given
- Project type and budget answers, verbatim
- Notes field: what they said they want, in their words
- Anything odd: a procurement address, an RFP mention, "full-time", "cheapest"

## 2. The company (three minutes)

- Website: what the product is, who it is for, whether it is live, a waitlist, or a deck
- Product state: sign-up page, pricing page, app store listings (iOS and Android), demo
- Age and size: founded year, team size, LinkedIn company page headcount
- Location and market: where they sell, which currency, which language
- Funding or news: a recent round, a launch, a press mention (search their name plus
  "raises", "launches", "announces")
- Hiring: open engineering roles are a capacity signal; "CTO wanted" is a strong one

## 3. The person (two minutes)

- LinkedIn: title, tenure, previous companies, whether technical or not
- Are they the decision maker? Founder or owner: yes. Product manager or ops: probably
  needs someone else in the room.
- Anything they have posted recently about the product or the problem

## 4. The existing product, if there is one (two minutes)

- What it is built on: page source hints (Webflow, Bubble, WordPress, Shopify, a framework),
  app store "developer" field, obvious no-code markers
- Visible problems: speed, broken flows, dated design, missing mobile
- Reviews: app store ratings and the top complaints
- Integrations they visibly use: payment provider, auth, chat widget, analytics

## 5. Competitors and reference points (one minute)

- One or two products they are obviously modelled on or competing with
- Whether QBLab's public case studies overlap with their sector (healthcare, e-commerce,
  consumer mobile)

## 6. Connection to QBLab (one minute)

- Shared contacts, previous conversations in email, an earlier booking, a referral source
- Whether they came from a specific post, page, or campaign, if any tracking says so

## Skip list

Do not spend time on: press about unrelated topics, the company's full history, personal
social media unrelated to work, or any attempt to guess revenue. If a fact needs more than
two searches, mark it "not found" and move on.
