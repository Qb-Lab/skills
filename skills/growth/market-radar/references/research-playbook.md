# Research playbook

Where to look, in order of how often it produces something actionable. Time box: about 30
minutes of research for a weekly run, 10 for a daily one. Stop when the box is spent and
list what was skipped.

## Sources by signal

| Signal | Where to look | What counts as a finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competitor moves | Their changelog, release notes, blog, pricing page, careers page, store "what's new", status page | A dated change: new feature, price or plan change, repositioned headline, a hire that signals direction, a removed feature |
| Complaints about competitors and substitutes | Review sites for the category, app-store reviews sorted by recent, community threads titled "alternative to", "switching from", "is X worth it" | The same complaint from several people in the last 90 days; a complaint this project already solves |
| Unmet needs | Questions with no good answer in forums and Q&A sites, feature-request boards, comment sections under the category's popular content | A need asked repeatedly, answered with a workaround |
| Adjacent products | Launch platforms, "new" lists in the store category, newsletters that cover the niche | A new entrant, or a neighbouring tool adding this project's core feature |
| Search and store movement | Autocomplete, related searches, comparison queries, store search suggestions and category charts | A new query shape ("X for <new use case>"), a competitor's brand being searched with "alternative" |
| Content patterns | The top recent posts and videos for the problem on the platforms in the context's Channels | A format or angle that several unrelated accounts got unusual reach with recently |
| Distribution openings | Newsletters taking submissions, directories, community showcase threads, marketplaces, integrations and partner programmes, events with open calls | A place the audience already gathers that accepts what this project can offer, with its rules and deadline |
| Platform and policy shifts | Platform announcements, store guideline updates, regulation that touches the category | A change with a date that helps or hurts a channel in the strategy |

Daily runs use only rows 1, 2 and 7, plus anything flagged `watch` with a date.

## Dating and grading evidence

- Record the date of the event, and the date you read it if they differ. An undated page
  is quoted as "undated, read <date>" and can support at most low confidence.
- Anything older than 90 days is background, not a signal, unless it has a deadline ahead.
- Primary sources (the competitor's own page, the platform's own announcement) outrank
  commentary about them. Read the primary source before reporting.
- Count independent sources. Ten replies in one thread are one source. Three threads in
  different communities are three.
- Engagement numbers are read from the page with the date ("412 upvotes, 2026-10-04"),
  never estimated, and never compared across platforms.
- A review-site rating is quoted with the number of reviews behind it.

## What qualifies as a trend

All three, or it is called an "observation":

1. Seen in at least three independent places.
2. Dated within the last 90 days, with more recent mentions than earlier ones.
3. Describable as a change ("teams are moving invoice capture from email plugins to
   standalone tools"), not a topic ("AI in accounting").

Never write "X is trending" from general knowledge. Never extrapolate a number.

## From observation to opportunity

Ask, in order:

1. Does this touch the primary segment in `audience.md`, or only the category at large?
2. Can the project respond with something it already has? If the response needs a feature
   that does not exist, it is product feedback: one line under "For the product", not an
   opportunity.
3. Is there a window? A competitor's price rise, an outage, a deprecation, a launch-week
   theme and an open call all expire.
4. What is the smallest response that tests it? Prefer a post, a reply, a comparison page
   or a single experiment over a campaign.

Good: "Competitor moved CSV export to its paid tier on 2026-09-28 (pricing page; two
threads complaining, links). We export free. Response: a 20-second export demo and a
plain comparison row on the landing page this week. Urgency: now. Confidence: high."

Bad: "Short-form video is growing in popularity; consider investing in video content."

## Things the radar does not do

- No sign-ups, logins, trial accounts or forms on competitor sites.
- No copying of competitor copy, visuals or a creator's script; describe the pattern.
- No claims about a competitor's revenue, user numbers or internal plans unless they
  published them.
- No disparagement to recommend: responses compare on checkable facts only.
