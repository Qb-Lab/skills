# Hooks

The first line of a post and the first second of a video decide whether the rest exists.
A hook is a true, specific reason to keep going. It is not a trick: the content has to pay
it off, and a hook the content cannot pay off is cut even if it would get the click.

Examples use made-up products. Do not copy them; copy the move.

## Patterns that work

| Pattern | The move | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Result first | open on the outcome, explain after | "This invoice took 4 seconds to enter. Here's the 11 minutes it used to take." |
| The specific problem | name the moment the audience recognises | "You export the CSV, fix the dates by hand, and import it again. Every Monday." |
| Curiosity gap | withhold one concrete thing | "We removed the signup form and trial starts went up. The reason is boring." |
| Number with a unit | a real figure, never rounded for effect | "37 playtesters quit on level 3. One wall was the problem." |
| Contrarian with a reason | a claim people will want to argue with, held for a real reason | "Streaks are why people quit habit apps. We removed ours." |
| Show, don't announce | start mid-demo | first frame: the finished ring on a hand, then the phone that designed it |
| Before / after | the two states side by side | "Left: our onboarding in March. Right: now. Same product." |
| Confession | something that went wrong, plainly | "I spent six weeks on a feature three people used." |
| Direct address to one group | call the exact reader | "If you run a clinic front desk, this is the no-show problem." |
| Question people already ask | their words, from the language bank | "How do you split rent when one room is bigger?" |

A number, a name or a result in a hook must come from the project's evidence. If the true
number is unimpressive, use a different pattern rather than a bigger number.

## Patterns to refuse

- Greetings and throat-clearing: "Hi guys, today I'm going to show you", "So I wanted to
  talk about", "Let me tell you a story".
- Announcements: "Big news", "We're excited to share", "It's finally here".
- Empty superlatives: "The best way to", "You won't believe", "This changes everything".
- Fake stakes and bait: "Nobody is talking about this", "Stop scrolling", "99% of people
  get this wrong" (unless there is a source for 99%).
- Generic questions: "Want to grow faster?", "Tired of wasting time?".
- A hook about the company instead of the reader or the thing.

## Text hooks

- The first line must stand alone, because most platforms cut the rest. Twelve words or
  fewer is a good ceiling.
- No hook should need the second line to make sense, and the second line must not repeat
  the first.
- Write five, keep the one that is most specific, not the one that is loudest.

## Video first frames

- Something is already happening in frame one: a hand, a screen mid-action, a result, a
  face mid-sentence. Never a logo, a title card or a person about to start talking.
- The on-screen text in the first second carries the hook for viewers with sound off; six
  words or fewer.
- Something visibly changes within about two seconds: a cut, a movement, a reveal. An
  uncut take is fine when the action itself moves. The payoff promised by the hook arrives
  before the viewer could reasonably doubt it will.
- For a product: show the most visual moment the product has (the context's "demonstrable
  in under 30 seconds" line), then rewind to how.

## Testing

Two hooks for the same piece are an experiment, not a preference. When a hook pattern
clearly outperforms on a channel, record it in `<state>/learnings.md`; when it clearly
fails, record that too.
