# Platform playbook

How each platform behaves, so content is made for it rather than pasted onto it. These are
durable behaviours, not algorithm trivia; specifications and limits change, so check the
platform's current limits before finalising anything length- or size-sensitive, and let
`<state>/learnings.md` overrule any line here.

## The adaptation rule

One idea, re-made per platform. Start again from the idea (the point, the evidence), not
from the previous platform's copy. What changes every time: the hook, the format, the
length, the register and the call to action. If two platforms would get the same text, one
of them should not get the post.

## Starting points, until the project has its own results

Cadence follows capacity first. These are opening guesses to test, not known-good values;
a calendar marks any slot based on them as untested, and the content log's results replace
them as soon as they exist.

| Platform | A sustainable start | Frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TikTok, Reels, Shorts | 3 short videos a week, the same shoot cut per platform | 9:16; keep text and faces out of the top strip and the bottom quarter, where the interface sits |
| Instagram feed | 1 or 2 carousels or images a week | 4:5; the cover must read as a still |
| LinkedIn | 2 or 3 posts a week, weekday mornings in the audience's time zone | 4:5 or 1:1 image; document carousels in portrait |
| X | a few posts a week plus daily replies | 16:9 or 1:1 media |
| Reddit, forums, communities | contribute weekly; post only when there is something worth a thread | text first |
| YouTube | one video every week or two | 16:9; thumbnail readable at phone size |
| Blog | 2 to 4 pieces a month | n/a |
| Email | one send per real piece of news; at most weekly | n/a |

Hashtags: zero to three specific ones where the platform uses them for discovery; none on
Reddit, Hacker News or email. Never a wall.

## TikTok

- Works: movement in the first second, one clear idea, demonstration, curiosity, humour, a
  real person or a real screen. Looks made on a phone.
- Shape: vertical 9:16, usually 15 to 40 seconds, on-screen text that carries the story
  with sound off, a caption that adds context rather than repeating.
- Hook: the result, the problem or the question, visible immediately. No intro.
- CTA: soft and native ("link in bio", "comment X and I'll send it", follow for part two).
- Punished: logos up front, slideshow adverts, landscape reuploads, watermarks from other
  apps, anything that feels like an advert before it has earned attention.

## Instagram

- Works: visual-first posts people save or send to a friend. Reels behave like TikTok with
  a slightly more polished look; carousels teach or tell a story in swipes; stories keep
  existing followers warm.
- Shape: Reels 9:16; carousels and feed 4:5; a first frame or cover that works as a still.
- Hook: the cover and the first slide do the work. One line of text on the cover at most.
- CTA: save, share, send to someone, link in bio.
- Punished: text-heavy graphics, engagement bait, stock imagery, hashtag walls.

## YouTube Shorts

- Works: the same cut as TikTok and Reels, with titles that are searchable. Good for
  education and "how to" because search keeps finding it.
- Shape: 9:16, under a minute; a title a person would type.
- CTA: point to the long video or the channel.

## YouTube (long form)

- Works: retention. A title and thumbnail that make one promise, an opening 30 seconds that
  proves the video will keep it, then structure: payoff early, sections, no preamble.
- Shape: 16:9; length set by the topic. Tutorials, teardowns, devlogs, comparisons.
- CTA: one, late, after value; the next video to watch.
- Punished: clickbait the video does not pay off, long intros, "like and subscribe" first.

## LinkedIn

- Works: a professional who sounds like a person. Specific stories from real work, build
  logs, lessons with the cost attached, opinions with a reason, case studies with numbers
  the author can stand behind.
- Shape: the first line is the whole hook (the rest is behind "see more"); short lines;
  under about 200 words for most posts; one image, a document carousel or a short native
  video when there is something to show.
- CTA: a question that invites a real answer, or a plain invite. Links tend to work better
  in a comment or in the profile than in the post body; test it.
- Punished: "thrilled to announce", humblebrags, broetry with one word per line, hashtag
  walls, engagement pods, tagging people who are not involved.

## X

- Works: a compressed idea, a clear opinion, a fast reaction to something happening now,
  build-in-public updates with a screenshot or a number, replies in other people's
  conversations (often worth more than original posts for a small account).
- Shape: one post that stands alone; a thread only when each post would survive alone;
  screenshots and short clips over link cards.
- CTA: rarely needed; reply, repost or a link in the last post of a thread.
- Punished: threads that open by announcing they are a thread, corporate voice, link-only
  posts.

## Reddit

- Works: being useful to that subreddit first. Detailed answers, honest write-ups of what
  was tried and what failed, numbers, lessons; founders speaking as themselves.
- Shape: text posts in the community's own style; long is fine when it is substance. Each
  subreddit has its own rules on self-promotion: read them before drafting.
- CTA: none in most cases. Mention the product only where it answers the question, and say
  plainly that it is yours.
- Punished (often with a ban): disguised adverts, new accounts that only post links, the
  same post in several subreddits, marketing language, asking for upvotes.

## Product Hunt

- Works: a maker telling the product's story to people who like trying new things.
- Shape: a tagline that says what it does in plain words, a gallery that shows the product
  working (a short video or GIF first), a first comment from the maker: why it was built,
  who it is for, what is unfinished, one question for the community.
- CTA: try it and give feedback. Be present in the comments all day.
- Punished: asking for upvotes (against the rules), vague taglines, launching with no way
  to try the product.

## Hacker News

- Works: something technical people can try or learn from, shown plainly. "Show HN" needs
  a working thing; write-ups need real detail.
- Shape: a factual title with no adjectives; the author answering questions in the thread.
- Punished: marketing language, vote rings, superlatives in titles, gated demos.

## Blog and search

- Works: one page per question the audience actually asks, with the answer in the first
  paragraph; comparisons, alternatives, how-tos, and data only this project has.
- Shape: the title in the searcher's words; headings as questions or plain claims; tables
  where there are comparisons.
- CTA: the next step that fits the reader's intent (try, see the demo, read the next page).
- Punished: keyword stuffing, pages that restate what ten other pages say.

## Email

- Works: one idea and one ask per email, written as a person to a person; a welcome email
  that gets the first useful action; launch notes to people who asked to hear.
- Shape: subject under about six words, plain, specific; body short enough to read on a
  phone without scrolling much.
- Punished: newsletters with five unrelated items, fake "Re:", sending to people who never
  opted in.

## Discord, Slack and forums

- Works: showing up consistently, answering questions, sharing work in progress in the
  channels meant for it. In your own community: give people something to do (feedback
  threads, playtests, office hours) before inviting them.
- Punished: drive-by links, DMs to members, cross-posting.
- The `community-growth` skill owns the rules of engagement.

## App Store and Google Play

- The listing is a landing page: the first two screenshots carry the pitch, the subtitle
  says what the app does in the user's words, the first sentence of the description is the
  only one most people read.
- Screenshots show the real app. Ratings prompts go after a moment of success.
- Keyword fields and titles: use the words from `<state>/audience.md`, not internal names.
