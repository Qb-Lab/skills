# Follow-up templates

## 1. The check-in

```
Subject: <Product name>, one month on

Hi <first name>,

<The observation: one sentence about something specific and true in their product right
now.>

How is it going on your side: <one question tied to their goal from the proposal, e.g. "are
the clinics using the schedule screen the way you hoped?">

Two small asks, both optional.

First, a line from you would help the next founder decide. Three questions make it easy:
what were you trying to do, what was it like working with us, and what happened after
launch? Two or three sentences is plenty; I will send it back for approval before it goes
anywhere.

Second, if you know one founder who needs a product live this quarter, an introduction
would mean a lot. I have put three lines below you can forward as they are.

<Owner name>
<Title>, QBLab
```

## 2. The thank-you reply (after they answer)

```
Subject: Re: <Product name>, one month on

Thank you, <first name>. Here is how I would quote it:

"<Two sentences in their words, lightly tidied, nothing added.>"
— <Name>, <Role>, <Company>

Is that right, and may I use it on qblab.co with your name and company? Happy to change
anything, or to use just a first name and sector if you prefer.

<Owner name>
```

## 3. The forwardable introduction

```
<Owner name> at QBLab built <product> for us: <what it is>, live in <N weeks / months>,
and we own all the code. If you have a product that needs to be live this quarter, book a
free 30-minute call and you leave with a plan and a fixed quote: <booking link>
```

## Notes

- One observation, one question, two asks, nothing else. Do not add a case-study pitch or
  an upsell.
- If the client is on the NDA list, drop the testimonial ask entirely and keep the referral
  ask.
- Subscription clients: replace the subject with "<Product name>, quarterly check-in" and
  skip the testimonial ask when one is already on file.
