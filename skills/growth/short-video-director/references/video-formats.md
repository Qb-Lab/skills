# Short video formats

Ten formats. Each: when it fits, the structure that works, what it needs from the owner.
Durations are starting points; a video is as long as its payoff needs and no longer. Check
the shared `platform-playbook.md` for per-platform differences before fixing a length.

## Screen-recording demo

**When:** the product does something visible in under 30 seconds. The default for software.
**Structure:** finished result first (1–2 s) → "here is how" → the three or four taps that
matter, sped up, zoomed to the active area → result again, now understood. 15–30 s.
**Needs:** a clean recording with realistic data, notifications off, zoom and cuts in edit.
**Fails when:** it starts on a login screen, shows every step, or the text is unreadable on
a phone.

## Talking head

**When:** an opinion, a lesson or a story where the person is the credibility. Requires
someone willing to be on camera.
**Structure:** the claim in the first sentence, mid-thought → one reason with a concrete
example → the turn or the cost → one line to take away. 20–45 s. Cut every breath.
**Needs:** phone at eye level, window light on the face, a real background, jump cuts or a
punch-in every few seconds, burned-in captions.
**Fails when:** it opens with a greeting or credentials, or reads a script word for word.

## UGC

**When:** consumer products; showing use inside a real day. Shot by the owner, a team
member or a real user with permission. Never a generated or implied customer.
**Structure:** the moment of need ("I had ten minutes before guests") → product in hand →
the result in the real setting → a reaction that is not acting. 12–25 s.
**Needs:** handheld phone, the real room, natural sound kept in, platform-native text.
**Fails when:** it is lit, scripted line by line, or tidied.

## Founder story

**When:** build-in-public, launches, a decision or mistake worth telling.
**Structure:** the stake or the number first ("We deleted our onboarding last week") → why
→ what happened, with one piece of evidence on screen → what is next or an open question.
30–60 s.
**Needs:** the founder's voice (to camera or as voice-over on real footage: commits, the
dashboard, the workshop), one true number the owner supplied.
**Fails when:** it is a victory lap, or the number is invented or rounded up.

## Before / after

**When:** the product changes a visible state: a messy inbox, a raw photo, a slow process.
**Structure:** the "before" held just long enough to hurt (2–3 s) → hard cut or wipe to
"after" → how, in one or two beats → optional split-screen. 10–20 s.
**Needs:** both states captured for real, in the same framing so the cut lands.
**Fails when:** the before is staged worse than reality, or the after appears first with no
contrast.

## Challenge

**When:** the product enables a task with a clear constraint: time, budget, number of steps.
**Structure:** the constraint as on-screen text and timer from frame one → attempt in fast
cuts → one thing going wrong → the outcome, pass or fail. 20–40 s.
**Needs:** a real attempt recorded in one go; failure kept in if it happened.
**Fails when:** the outcome is obviously rigged or the constraint is not visible.

## Reaction

**When:** there is a real artefact to react to: a user's message, a review, a competitor
claim, a common bad practice. Only with the right to show it; anonymise people.
**Structure:** the artefact on screen with the key line highlighted → the reaction in one
sentence → the substance: what is true, shown in the product or with evidence. 15–35 s.
**Needs:** the source (screenshot with names removed, or stitched clip where the platform
allows it and the creator's settings permit).
**Fails when:** it mocks a person, or reacts to something fabricated.

## Educational

**When:** the audience has a recurring question the owner can answer better than the top
results. Builds saves and trust rather than installs.
**Structure:** the mistake or the surprising answer first → three steps at most, each shown
not told → recap card worth saving. 25–50 s.
**Needs:** a specific, narrow topic ("the one column that breaks CSV imports"), on-screen
steps, proof the method works.
**Fails when:** it is a list of seven tips, or the product is forced into every step.

## Trend adaptation

**When:** a format or sound verified live this week genuinely fits the product's point.
From a dated `market-radar` entry, never from memory.
**Structure:** the trend's own structure, beat for beat, with the project's twist at the
point the trend expects one. Length matches the trend.
**Needs:** the verified reference (link and date), turnaround within days, humour allowed
in the context.
**Fails when:** the trend is old, the fit is forced, or another creator's script is copied.

## Comedy / skit

**When:** the pain is universal in the audience and funnier acted than explained. Context
must allow humor.
**Structure:** the recognisable situation in the first shot → escalation in two beats → the
turn (product as the relief, shown briefly or not at all) → out on the laugh, no CTA. 15–30 s.
**Needs:** one or two people playing roles with a prop each (hat, glasses), tight cuts.
**Fails when:** the product pitch arrives before the joke, or the joke needs explaining.

## Choosing quickly

| Situation | Format |
| --- | --- |
| Nobody on camera, software product | screen-recording demo, before/after |
| Nobody on camera, physical product | hands-only UGC, before/after |
| Founder willing, B2B | talking head, founder story |
| Consumer app, shareable result | UGC, challenge |
| Strong objection to overcome | reaction, educational |
| Game | before/after of a moment, challenge, raw gameplay clip treated as a demo |
