# Ideal client profile

Use this to qualify leads, choose who to reach out to, and decide what to say first.

## Good fit (from the site's "Good fit" block)

- Has a product to build and wants it live this quarter, not next year
- Can make decisions quickly and is available for a short weekly check-in
- Wants one team to design, build, deploy and maintain it
- Would rather own the code and the accounts than rent them

## Not a fit (from the site's "Not a fit" block)

- Needs a large team on site full-time
- Scope is not decided and they want to find it by building for a year
- Is looking for the cheapest possible hourly rate
- Needs a certified vendor for a regulated procurement process

## Who they usually are

- A founder or business owner with an idea, a spec, a deck, or a half-built product that is
  stuck
- A non-technical operator who needs a working product, not a technology conversation
- A small company replacing an agency, a freelancer, or an internal tool that stopped
  scaling
- Occasionally a technical lead who needs a whole product delivered while their team is
  busy elsewhere

Sectors QBLab has shipped in (public case studies): healthcare / telehealth, e-commerce with
AI recommendations, consumer meal-planning on web and mobile. Treat these as evidence, not
as limits.

## Signals worth noticing during research

Positive:

- They have a live product, a landing page, or a waitlist: something exists to build on
- They mention a deadline, a launch, a funding round, or a customer waiting
- They are hiring developers or have an open "CTO / lead engineer" role: they need capacity
- Their current product is visibly slow, broken, or built on a no-code tool they have outgrown
- They asked for a mobile app plus a backend plus a dashboard: a complete product

Caution:

- Budget answer on the booking form is far below any plausible project size
- The request is a single small task (a bug fix, a plugin): possible, but not the model
- A large enterprise domain, a procurement email address, or an RFP attachment
- "We need a developer to join our team full-time on site"
- "Can you build me a copy of X" with no angle of their own

## Questions that decide fit

Ask these on the call when research could not answer them:

1. What exists today, and who uses it?
2. What has to be true in three months for this to have been worth it?
3. Who makes the decisions, and how fast?
4. Is there a date that matters (launch, investor, customer, event)?
5. Web, mobile, or both, and why?
6. What has to integrate with it (payments, auth, an existing system)?
7. Who will own and run it after launch?
