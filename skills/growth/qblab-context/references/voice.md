# Voice

QBLab writes the way a senior engineer explains something to a founder over coffee: plain,
specific, calm, and short. The site is the reference; match it.

## Rules

1. **Sentence case everywhere.** Headlines, buttons, labels. No Title Case, no ALL CAPS.
2. **Short sentences with a verb.** One idea per sentence. Cut the second clause.
3. **Concrete over abstract.** "Most requests delivered in 2–5 days", not "rapid turnaround".
   "You own the code and the accounts", not "full ownership and transparency".
4. **Say the mechanism, not the adjective.** Not "fast, reliable development" but "AI does the
   fast part; a senior engineer reviews every change."
5. **Name the fear, then answer it.** Founders worry about lock-in, surprises, and being
   sold to. Address those directly ("If you stop working with us, nothing changes").
6. **No eyebrow labels, no arrows on buttons, no exclamation marks, no emoji** in anything
   that represents the brand.
7. **No prices, no promises of outcomes, no invented numbers.** See `proof.md`.
8. **Second person, present tense.** "You describe the product. We ask the questions that
   change the estimate."
9. **Honest labels.** Demo is a demo. Collaborator is a collaborator. In progress is in
   progress.
10. **End on the next step.** Client-facing copy ends with the call: "Book a free 30-min
    call. Leave with a plan and a fixed quote."

## Examples from the site (copy this register)

- "Your product, live in weeks. Not months."
- "Working software every few days, not a reveal at the end."
- "You see what will be built before anything is built."
- "Because every product is different and a number on a page would be a guess."
- "You can pause or cancel at any time. Anything in progress is finished and handed over."

## Banned words and phrases

cutting-edge · state-of-the-art · leverage · synergy · seamless · robust (as filler) ·
world-class · best-in-class · passionate · innovative · disruptive · digital transformation ·
unlock · empower · elevate · revolutionise · game-changer · end-to-end (say "complete") ·
solutions (say what it is) · "we're excited to" · "in today's fast-paced world" · "AI-powered"
as a headline · "10x" · any claim with "guaranteed" that is not the review guarantee.

If a sentence needs one of these to work, the sentence is the problem.

## Channel notes

- **LinkedIn:** first line is the whole hook; no "I'm thrilled to announce". Show the thing
  shipped and how long it took. One idea per post. End with a question or a plain invite,
  not a hashtag wall (three hashtags at most).
- **Email and outreach:** subject line under six words. First sentence is about them, not
  QBLab. One ask per message. Sign as a person, not "The QBLab Team".
- **Proposals and briefs:** tables for scope and timeline, prose for reasoning, numbers on
  their own line. Assumptions and exclusions written out.
