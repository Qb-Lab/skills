# Visual modes

Pick one per asset. Mixing two (editorial lighting on a UGC concept) is the fastest way to
make something look generated. Each mode lists when it fits, the cues that make it read as
real, and what breaks it.

## UGC

**When:** consumer products on TikTok, Reels, Stories; anything meant to look like a user
showed a friend. Best when the owner or a real user can simply shoot it.
**Cues:** phone main lens at arm's length or propped on something; vertical; slightly too
close; horizon a few degrees off; mixed light (window plus a warm bulb); a real room with
stuff in it (charging cable, mug, laundry chair); thumb or forearm entering frame; phone
HDR look, no shallow depth of field; nobody looking at the lens unless it is a selfie.
**Breaks it:** studio softness, centred symmetry, a tidy desk, matching outfits, a logo in
the corner, colour grading, any caption font that is not the platform's own.

## Editorial

**When:** launch hero images, brand campaigns, premium positioning, press kits.
**Cues:** one idea per frame; a deliberate, restrained palette (two colours plus neutrals);
directional light with real shadows; generous negative space for a headline; styling that
looks chosen by a person (fabric creases, an off-centre object); texture and grain.
**Breaks it:** everything lit evenly, three concepts in one frame, glossy gradients as a
background, lens flare, a model smiling at the product.

## Product

**When:** ecommerce, physical goods, app-store art, comparison content.
**Cues:** the product at true scale on a real surface (wood, linen, concrete, a hand for
size); one key light and a fill, visible contact shadow; honest material rendering
(scratches on metal, a seam on fabric); for packshots a plain background with a real floor
line; for digital products the device is a prop and the screen is the subject.
**Breaks it:** floating objects with no shadow, mirror-floor reflections, impossible
materials, a device model that does not exist, the product larger than physics allows.

## Founder

**When:** build-in-public posts, B2B trust, hiring, milestones. Only with the real founder,
and only if the context's content rules allow it.
**Cues:** the actual workplace as it is (second monitor with real work, a whiteboard that
was used, a kitchen table); candid mid-task, not arms crossed; available light; shot by a
colleague at eye level. A slightly bad photo of the real person beats any generated one.
**Breaks it:** generated faces (never), stock offices with glass walls, headset-and-smile
poses, a suit the founder does not own.

## Documentary

**When:** customer stories, behind the scenes, events, field products, communities.
**Cues:** observational distance; subjects absorbed in what they are doing; context in the
frame (the queue, the workshop, the stall); natural light, motion blur allowed; a sequence
of three frames tells more than one hero.
**Breaks it:** posing, retouching, removing the mess, a generated scene captioned as a real
event. This mode is shot, not generated; without real footage choose another mode.

## Meme

**When:** culture and trend responses, community humour, where the audience already speaks
this way and the context allows humour.
**Cues:** the native format exactly as the internet uses it (screenshot with crop marks,
low-res reaction image, plain top text, notes-app screenshot); visibly low effort; one joke;
the brand absent or tiny.
**Breaks it:** polish, a brand colour scheme, a logo lockup, explaining the joke, using a
format that peaked long ago, using an identifiable person's image without the right to.

## UI / product demo

**When:** developer tools, SaaS, any feature announcement, comparison and how-to content.
**Cues:** a real screenshot or recording from the running product with realistic data
(plausible names, uneven numbers, a few items not a perfect grid); cropped to the one area
that matters; annotation with one arrow or highlight; device frame only when the platform
context needs it; cursor mid-action.
**Breaks it:** redrawn or generated interface, lorem ipsum, screens floating at angles in
space, glow, a browser bar showing localhost, personal data left in view.

## Lifestyle

**When:** showing the product inside a believable day: consumer apps, wearables, food,
fitness, travel.
**Cues:** the product is in use but not the centre (phone in hand at a bus stop, app open
beside a pan on the hob); the person's attention is on their task; the location matches the
audience's actual geography and housing; ordinary clothes; weather that exists.
**Breaks it:** everyone delighted, a spotless open-plan loft, a laptop on a beach, the
screen angled perfectly at the camera, diversity arranged like a brochure.

## Choosing

| Objective | First choice | Fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stop the scroll on short-video platforms | UGC | meme |
| Show the product working | UI / product demo | product |
| Make a claim believable | documentary or founder | UI demo with real data |
| Launch or brand moment | editorial | product |
| Recognition ("that is me") | lifestyle | UGC |
