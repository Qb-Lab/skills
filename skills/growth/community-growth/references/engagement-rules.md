# Engagement rules

## The ladder

| Rung | What the owner does | What is allowed | Move up when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Contribute | Answers questions, shares what they know, no product | No mention, no link, nothing in the bio doing the selling for them | A few weeks of replies people thanked or upvoted |
| 2. Build trust | Posts something the community keeps: a teardown, a template, real numbers, a mistake and what it cost | Mention of "what I work on" only if asked | Members reply to them by name, or a moderator engages |
| 3. Earn attention | People ask what they are building | Answer the question asked, with disclosure, no pitch | It happens more than once without prompting |
| 4. Mention when relevant | Someone asks for exactly this, or a showcase thread exists | One disclosed mention that also names when the product is the wrong choice | Never automatic; each mention is judged on its own |

Standing maps to rungs: `none` and `lurker` are rung 1, `contributor` is rung 2 or 3,
`known` is rung 4. Roughly nine useful contributions for every one that touches the
product is a sensible ratio to hold; it is a guide for the owner, not a quota to hit.

## Promo risk

| Risk | When | What the draft may contain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| low | Rules allow it (showcase thread, "what are you building" post), or the person asked for a tool like this, and standing is `contributor` or better | Disclosed mention, one link if links are allowed |
| medium | Relevant thread, rules silent or "limited self-promotion", standing `contributor` | The useful answer; the product named only as "the thing I build does X" without a link, or left for a follow-up if asked |
| high | Rules forbid promotion, rules not read, standing `none` or `lurker`, a thread about a competitor's failure, a person in distress | No mention. Contribute or skip. |

When unsure, it is `high`. A ban costs every future conversation in that community.

## Writing the reply

- Answer the question in the first sentence. The answer must be complete without the
  product.
- Write the way the thread is written: its length, its formality, its vocabulary. No
  greeting, no sign-off, no "great question", no bullet-pointed brochure.
- Bring something only a practitioner has: a number the owner really measured, a failure,
  a step people skip. If the owner has nothing to add, skip the thread.
- Disclosure is one plain clause: "I build <product>, so discount this accordingly".
- Say when the product is not the right fit. It is the fastest way to be believed.
- No link unless the link is the answer and the rules allow it. Never a tracking link, a
  shortened link or a referral code.
- Never criticise a competitor by name beyond a checkable fact.

Bad (high risk, will be removed):

> Great question! You should check out LedgerLift, an AI-powered platform that streamlines
> invoice processing. Sign up free at …

Good (medium risk, standing contributor):

> The duplicates usually come from the same invoice arriving as a PDF and again as a
> forwarded email, so matching on invoice number alone misses them. Match on supplier +
> amount + date within three days and you catch most. I build an extraction tool so I've
> stared at this a lot; happy to share the matching rules if useful, they work in a
> spreadsheet too.

## Topics worth posting

A post earns its place when the community would have wanted it even if the product did not
exist. Shapes that work: a teardown of a real problem with the working shown; data the
owner collected, with the method; a mistake with its cost; a free template or small tool; a
well-formed question the owner honestly wants answered. Each uses only facts the owner can
stand behind; no invented numbers, no borrowed stories.

## What gets accounts removed

Dropping a link as a first post · the same reply in several threads · replying to old
threads only where the product fits · undisclosed affiliation · friends or staff posing as
customers · asking for upvotes · direct messages to people who did not ask · arguing with
moderators · surveys that are lead capture · "feedback wanted" posts that are launches in
disguise.

## Platform notes

Read the shared `platform-playbook.md` for each platform's native behaviour. In short:
Reddit punishes anything that reads as marketing and checks post history; Discord and
Slack run on presence over weeks and have channel-specific rules; forums and niche sites
value long, complete answers; LinkedIn and Facebook groups vary by moderator and many are
vendor-heavy, so check who actually replies; on X the opening is a reply that adds to the
conversation, not a quote that redirects it.
